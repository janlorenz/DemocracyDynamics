## Core assumption 

A perfect democracy fitting for all societies is impossible. (We can trace this back to impossibility theorems.) It is also complicated to assess which democratic system is better. Not only because it is multidimensional, but also because a well functioning democratic system in one society may fit bad in another society. Further on, there are fundamental conflicts of democratic principles where nuanced balance is needed. 
Aspects of a democratic systems which have been perceived as functioning well can also degenerate through behavioral adaptations of the political actors. Are there self stabilizing or self-improving dynamics of triggered by behavioral adaptation of political actors? Let us try do identify them. Let us explore how resilient these improving paths are. Can they come off track from virtuous to vicious cycles? How? By some random fluctuation? Through nudges by malicious players? What else? How can they come back? How can we monitor it? 
Next challenge: Sometimes the right adaptation to improve democratic quality may lie in a reform of the democratic system. However, in a democracy, reforms must come from within the system. Can we build mechanisms of system reforms into a democratic system? How can we assess if such built in reforms of itself work? 
Types of reforms: What is a reform practically? It can be a change of constitution. It can be a new law or change of a fundamental law regulating democratic processes. It can be a verdict of a constitutional court. It is also a conscious public action driven by a substantial group of core actors through self-commitment or codex.  

## Aspects of Democratic Systems - Listing Pages

### Theoretical Aspects of Democracy

- **Democratic Principles.** 
	Definition: A democratic principle is something which is considered essential for a  democracy by most experts and lay persons. Academically, democratic principles are treated in political philosophy, political theory, and constitutional law. Democratic principles imply guidelines and quality criteria for the design of political and legal institutions, and the judgment of democratic culture and practices. By their very nature a multitude of practical implementations of democratic principles can easily create contradictions needing some trade off. Nevertheless, democratic principles are the essence of the very idea of democracy. 
- **Powers / Branches of the State**
	Definition: A power or branch of a democracy is an abstract entity of power which has a final authority over decisions about specific matters with clear limitations on other matters. The specification of these powers is central to the principle of the separation of powers (checks and balances). Powers are typically executed by institutions, the abstract power concept goes beyond that, several institutions can be involved.

### Practical Aspects of Democratic

- **Case Stories** (Historical Stories)
	Definition: All the principles and powers in a concept of democracy remain abstract and theoretical when we do not observe, explain, and scrutinize them in existing or historic democratic systems. Case stories highlight how different powers and decision processes were created, implemented, and maintained with different constitutions, institutions and practices, building on the history and culture of nations, states, and other self-governing entities.  
- **Democratic Innovations**
	Definition: A democratic innovation is a concrete proposal to establish a new institution and/or process of collective decision making. In some sense it is a hypothetical case story, outlining a modified democractic system which does not yet exist. 
- **Data Stories.**
	Definition: Democracies and democratic processes digitize. In a democracy adhering to transparency and accountability, more and more data accumulates automatically in official records of parliaments, governments, courts, and public administration. Further on, also citizens and politicians leave digital traces about the political activities in social media, traditional news media, voting advice applications, and so on.  Moreover, political science and other empirical social sciences provide a rich reservoir of curated data (such as multidimensional democracy indices) as well as surveys of citizens about their political life and political attitudes, beliefs and preferences (often large scale international and representative through random selection). Analysis of such data can deliver insights about the state and fate of democracies by creatively finding new insights and providing overview (which is often hidden in political communication behind strategically picked data or their biased interpretation).  communicated data points). 
- **Mechanism Explorables**
	Definition: The design of a specific mechanism and algorithm for aggregating a collective decision can be a challenge in itself, when they should adhere to a multitude of democratic principles which are at least in part in contradiction. The problems range from impossibility theorems in social and public choice, over dilemmas, trilemmas and the like, to sophisticated algorithmic design questions. Such problems need to be understood for a proper design of institutions and decision processes. Moreover, the decisions by the different democratic powers happen repeatedly and not in isolation of the other powers. So, each decision changes the conditions for the self-organization of actors in a democratic systems. This complex dynamical system is creates an ever changing political reality. Complex systems can show emergent properties through systemic mechanisms hidden in the repeated interaction of actors. Such mechanisms should be understood to better design, organize, and reform democracy avoiding unintended consequences, anticipating vicious cycles, and fuelig virtuous cycles. Mechanisms of collective decisions and systemic mechanisms in complex systems are best explored and understood with interactive explorables which allow to play with algorithms and systems.

### Democratic principles

- Representation and Legitimacy of Representatives
	Repräsentative Demokratie: Ein wichtiges Merkmal ist die demokratische Auswahl von Repräsentanten die dann in den Institution demokratische legitimierte Entscheidungen treffen und Macht ausüben. Eine wichtige Frage ist also, welche Repräsentanten wie ausgewählt werden. Philipp Amthor (Spiegel, 31.8.2026): »Die Grundidee der repräsentativen Demokratie muss es doch sein, dass die Schlauesten, Fleißigsten und Robustesten die Politik gestalten«, sagt Amthor. Auch deswegen ging er in die Politik. »Wenn man die Gabe hat, schlauer und robuster als andere zu sein, hat man doch eine Verpflichtung, sich in das Ganze miteinzubringen«, sagt Amthor. »Da muss doch der Leistungsgedanke zugrunde liegen.«
- Popular sovereignty
	direct vs. representative expression (referenda vs. parliamentary mandate), constituent power vs. constituted power (who can rewrite the constitution itself), popular sovereignty vs. constitutional constraints (can "the people" vote away their own rights?), the EU's democratic-deficit debate (sovereignty pooled above the nation-state), plebiscitary abuse (authoritarian leaders invoking "the people" to bypass institutions).
- Protection of civil liberties
	speech vs. hate speech regulation (US vs. European/German approaches), religious freedom vs. secularism (laïcité debates), assembly rights vs. public order restrictions, digital-age liberties (surveillance, data privacy as a modern civil liberty), balancing liberties against security post-9/11 or during emergencies (Ausnahmezustand / state-of-emergency powers).
- Human rights
	universalism vs. cultural relativism, positive vs. negative rights (freedom from vs. entitlement to), justiciability (which rights courts can enforce vs. aspirational/programmatic rights), international human rights law vs. national sovereignty (ECHR rulings binding domestic law), horizontal effect (do rights bind private actors/corporations, not just the state?).
- Rule of law
	formal vs. substantive rule of law (mere legality vs. law that is just), Rechtsstaat vs. rule by law (authoritarian regimes that follow their own laws while being undemocratic), retroactivity and legal certainty, judicial independence as enforcement mechanism, current "rule of law crises" (Poland, Hungary — EU Article 7 proceedings) as case studies in backsliding.
- Separation of powers (checks and balances)
	presidential vs. parliamentary systems (fusion vs. separation of executive/legislative), judicial review's countermajoritarian tension (unelected courts striking down elected legislatures' laws), fourth-branch institutions (central banks, electoral commissions, audit courts), executive overreach via emergency powers or decree authority, party discipline eroding separation in parliamentary systems (executive and legislative majority controlled by same party).
- Free and fair elections
	electoral systems and their effects (proportional vs. first-past-the-post, gerrymandering), campaign finance regulation, voter suppression vs. voter-ID integrity measures, election observation and international standards (OSCE/ODIHR benchmarks), disinformation and foreign interference in the digital-campaign era.
- Political equality
	formal equality ("one person, one vote") vs. substantive equality (does money/media access distort real influence?), weighted representation debates (US Senate malapportionment, federal chamber design), suffrage history and expansion (property, race, gender, age qualifications), representation of underrepresented groups (quotas, reserved seats), non-citizen/resident voting rights.
- Majority rule with Minority rights
	constitutional supermajority requirements to entrench minority protections, consociational/power-sharing democracy (Lijphart — Belgium, Bosnia, Lebanon) as an alternative to simple majoritarianism, judicial review as a countermajoritarian safeguard, tyranny-of-the-majority historical cases, minority veto rights vs. governability trade-offs.
- Institutional accountability and transparency
	horizontal vs. vertical accountability (O'Donnell's framework), freedom-of-information law design and its limits (state secrets, national security exceptions), independent oversight bodies (ombudspersons, auditors-general, anti-corruption agencies), lobbying registries and revolving-door regulation, whistleblower protection vs. state-secrecy claims.
- Free press and civil society
	media ownership concentration and pluralism law, public broadcasting independence vs. state capture, platform/social-media regulation (disinformation, algorithmic amplification), NGO "foreign agent" laws as tools of civil-society suppression (Russia, Hungary as cases), press freedom indices and their methodology, journalist safety and SLAPP suits (strategic lawsuits against public participation).
- Free and pluralistic party formation (parties as emergent institutions)
	ballot-access thresholds as barriers to entry (are signature/vote thresholds fair gatekeeping or entrenchment of incumbents?), party financing and dependency on state subsidy vs. private donors, internal party democracy requirements (candidate selection transparency), the tension with militant democracy (when does regulating a party become suppressing legitimate opposition?), dominant-party systems that retain formal pluralism without genuine competition (electoral authoritarianism, e.g., historically Mexico's PRI or Singapore's PAP).
- Stateness (basic order / bounded political community) 
	secession procedures (Québec's clarity requirements, Scotland's referendum-based approach, Ethiopia's constitutional secession right vs. its non-use), contested statehood, minority self-determination vs. territorial integrity, federalism as a stateness-management tool
- Monopoly on the legitimate use of force 
	civilian control of the military (constitutional design — who can order troop deployment, budget oversight), police accountability and judicial oversight, prohibition of party militias/private armies, coup-proofing, the tension between security-service secrecy and democratic transparency (parallel to your accountability/transparency principle above).
- Peaceful transfer of power
	concession norms and their erosion (informal norm vs. legal requirement — what happens when a loser refuses to concede, e.g., contested elections and post-election crises), constitutional succession mechanisms (interim government provisions, caretaker governments during transitions), the role of the military as guarantor vs. threat (militaries that enforce peaceful transfer vs. militaries that stage coups to prevent it), certification and dispute-resolution bodies (electoral courts, who has final authority to declare a winner), historical near-failures and successes as case studies (transitions during civil unrest, contested counts, coalition-formation deadlocks in parliamentary systems).
- Defensive/Militant democracy
	party-ban jurisprudence and its thresholds (Germany's NPD case — unconstitutional aims but "too insignificant" to ban, Turkey's frequent party closures as a contrast in overuse), the paradox of tolerance (Popper) vs. free-association absolutism (US First Amendment approach as the rejection case), who decides — courts vs. legislatures vs. executive (risk of incumbents weaponizing "defense of democracy" against genuine opposition), scope beyond parties (militant-democracy logic applied to hate speech law, extremist civil-service bans like Germany's Radikalenerlass, proscription of organizations/movements short of parties), historical origin and justification (Loewenstein's 1937 response to Weimar's legal collapse) vs. contemporary application to digital-age extremism and disinformation-driven anti-democratic movements.
- Economic freedom bound to public good (as liberal-democratic principle)
	property as a check on state power (economic independence enabling political dissent) vs. property as entrenching pre-existing inequality into political influence (money in politics, oligarchic capture — tying back to your political-equality principle); social-market economy vs. laissez-faire vs. socialist-democratic models as different resolutions of the freedom/public-good balance; expropriation and compensation law (when can the state take property, and on what terms — Art. 14(3) GG as a case study); the welfare state as the institutional mechanism binding markets to public good (redistribution, regulation); economic democracy debates (co-determination/Mitbestimmung — should workers have governance rights in firms, not just political ones); Piketty-style debates on whether extreme wealth concentration is itself a threat to political equality and democratic legitimacy.
- Federations, principle of subsidiarity, local autonomy
	Global vs. local, responsibility and accountability
- Taxation and budgeting as core topics of democrativ decision and legitimacy
	Wealth vs. income; wealth distribution; efficiency, transparency, fairness of taxation; efficiency and transparency of spending. Participatory budgeting. Prevention of vicious cycles. Triggering of positive economic dynamics. Budget tension of captial vs. current expenditure (investive vs. consumptive). Distinguish according to IMF GFS Manual
		1. **Revenue** – _taxes, social contributions, grants received, other revenue (property income, sales, fines)._
		2. **Expense** (deine Punkte 1–8) – _current spending that does not create an asset._
			1. Compensation of employees – _wages and salaries paid to government staff_
			2. Use of goods and services – _day-to-day operating costs, incl. maintenance_
			3. Consumption of fixed capital – _depreciation of existing assets_
			4. Interest – _interest paid on government debt_
			5. Subsidies – _payments to enterprises to lower their costs or prices_
			6. Grants – _transfers to other governments or international organizations_
			7. Social benefits – _pensions, unemployment benefits, and other social support_
			8. Other expense – _residual current expenses not classified elsewhere_
			-> **Net operating balance** = Revenue − Expense _(a measure of whether current operations are sustainable)_
		3. **Net acquisition of nonfinancial assets** (Investment) – _purchases minus sales of fixed assets, inventories, and other nonfinancial assets._
			1. Acquisitions of fixed assets – _buying or building capital assets (roads, buildings, equipment)_
			2. Disposals of fixed assets – _selling existing capital assets_
			-> **Net lending/net borrowing** = Net operating balance − Net acquisition of nonfinancial assets _(shows whether government is a net lender or borrower to the rest of the economy)_
		4. **Transactions in financial assets and liabilities** – _how the deficit is financed or the surplus is used: borrowing, issuing debt, buying/selling shares, changing cash reserves._
